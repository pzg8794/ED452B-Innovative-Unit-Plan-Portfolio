# Theoretical Framework

The unit is grounded in three connected lenses: Karten / MTSS, Universal Design for Learning, and culturally sustaining asset-based computer science pedagogy.

## 1. Karten, Inclusion, and MTSS

Karten's inclusion framework supports proactive instructional design. The unit does not treat accommodations as separate from instruction. Instead, supports are embedded into the whole-class environment and intensified when student evidence shows a targeted need.

### Unit applications

- Tier 1 visual click-paths for all students.
- NPC read-aloud and task-goal restatement before coding.
- Stop-Read-Predict-Run-Debug protocol.
- Driver/Navigator roles for structured collaboration.
- Planning sheets before longer code sequences.
- Multiple modes of response: code, oral explanation, drawing, exit ticket, and teacher conference.

## 2. Universal Design for Learning

UDL guides the unit through multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression.

### Engagement

Minecraft is motivating for many students, but engagement is not enough. The unit adds predictable routines, bounded choices, extension roles, and an error-normalizing debugging culture.

### Representation

Students encounter coding concepts through projected teacher modeling, icons, vocabulary cards, physical sequence cards, NPC read-alouds, screenshots, and peer talk.

### Action and expression

Students show learning through code, planning sheets, debugging notes, oral explanations, partner teach-backs, teacher conferences, and final reflection.

## 3. Asset-Based Computer Science Pedagogy

The unit treats prior Minecraft knowledge, visual-spatial reasoning, peer explanation, creative building, home-language resources, and oral demonstration as assets. Students who already know Minecraft are not treated as distractions; they can become Expert Helpers who ask questions and support peers without taking over.

## 4. Functional Assessment Lens

The unit also reflects the Session 8 functional-assessment principle that assessment should identify strengths, supports, and success conditions, not only problems. In this unit, a student who struggles with the NPC text is not automatically treated as a weak coder. The teacher asks:

- What is the barrier?
- What support changed participation?
- What evidence shows the student understands the concept?
- What routine should be redesigned next time?

## Design Implication

The theoretical framework leads to a practical assessment stance:

> Completion is not enough. Planning, prediction, debugging, explanation, and productive use of supports are all evidence of learning.
