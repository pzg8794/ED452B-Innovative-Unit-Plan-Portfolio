# Context and Rationale

## Setting

The unit is designed for Grade 4 students in the IGNITE Computer Science and Digital Literacy rotation at Pine Brook Elementary. Students enter the Minecraft Education sequence with varied digital fluency, reading stamina, keyboard and mouse control, prior Minecraft knowledge, executive-functioning skills, regulation needs, and language backgrounds.

## Why Context Matters

Minecraft Education can make computer science visible and motivating, but it also introduces layered access demands. Students must log in, find the correct world, interpret NPC directions, use keyboard controls, open MakeCode, construct block code, test the Agent, and revise when the code does not work.

For inclusive planning, these are not minor details. They determine whether student understanding becomes visible.

## Placement-Based Rationale

TeachingPlacement evidence showed several recurring barriers:

- Students skipped or misread NPC directions.
- Students misunderstood the goal of the dual-gold-plate challenge.
- Students needed clarification that the Agent and student could stand on separate plates.
- Students benefited from specific hints such as counting spaces or remembering that the Agent can float.
- Some students could complete steps but struggled to explain the computational reasoning.
- Some students with prior Minecraft experience needed extension work to stay productively engaged.

## Core Design Response

The final unit redesigns Minecraft coding instruction around one principle:

> Do not confuse access barriers with computational-thinking deficits.

This means the unit intentionally separates:

- interface access from coding knowledge;
- reading load from task understanding;
- impulsive clicking from conceptual misunderstanding;
- partner dominance from collaboration;
- final task completion from debugging and explanation.

## Target Learners

The unit specifically plans for three target-student profiles:

1. **Executive-functioning profile** - strong ideas but needs support with planning, reading before clicking, and slowing down before running code.
2. **CLD / language-processing profile** - understands concepts more readily through visual, oral, and peer-supported routes than through dense English-only NPC text.
3. **Social-communication / regulation profile** - works best when routines, roles, expectations, and transitions are predictable.

## Rationale for Innovation

The innovation is not simply using Minecraft. The innovation is using Minecraft as an inclusive CS environment where supports are designed into the learning sequence. Students learn algorithms, sequencing, debugging, loops, and efficiency through a high-interest platform while also learning routines for planning, collaboration, reflection, and self-advocacy.
